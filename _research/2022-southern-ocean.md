The Southern Ocean is a disproportionately large sink for anthropogenic carbon, so its biogeochemical state is a leading indicator for climate monitoring. The measurement record, however, is sparse and irregularly sampled — the regime where kernel methods still beat deep networks.

* **Forecasted** key Southern Ocean indicators, including pH (25 °C) and nitrate concentration, using support vector regression for climate monitoring applications.
* **Applied** correlation-based feature selection to isolate the oceanographic variables that carry genuine predictive signal.
* **Proposed** two novel custom kernels — a Hyperbolic Sine kernel and a Gaussian Matrix Multiplier kernel — and **demonstrated** superior predictive performance over the standard RBF, polynomial and sigmoid kernels on this data.
